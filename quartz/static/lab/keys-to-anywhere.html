Keys to Anywhere

Think of every arrangement of a few bullets as a place, and every key press that moves a bullet as a step between two places. This page maps that space for outlines of up to five bullets. It shows how many presses it takes to get from one outline to another, which keys the space can do without, and what the two answers to decision 18 (a key that carries a bullet out of its list, or a command that names the destination) would change.

Every number comes from a small model written for this page: it moves a bullet, with what is under it, on labelled outlines. It is not the plugin’s code. For today’s four keys, the plugin’s own code gives the same outline as the model on every key press at three, four and five bullets measured (plugin commit 4f06fe3; see Where It Lands). Outlines are drawn as Markdown source, two spaces per level.

measuredWe counted it, with a script, on the model of chapter 1.
reportedA note in the library or a paper says it.

  2. A change of the whole outline can take far more: up to – presses for five bullets. measured

A command with a typed destination makes every move one command. The model counts presses and nothing else about the two options. measured
  4. Of the 511 combinations of the nine keys, – reach every outline from every other, and every one of them contains indent. The smallest have two keys: indent with a move up, or down, that leaves its list. The plain move keys need both indent and outdent. measured

1 · the model

An outline is a place, a key press is a step

An outline here is a forest of labelled bullets, a, b, c and so on, each with a place: its parent and its position among that parent’s children. Three bullets can be arranged ways, four ways and five ways. A key press takes one bullet, with everything under it, to a new place. Nine keys are modelled:

  • indent (Tab): the bullet becomes the last child of the bullet above it.
  • outdent (Shift-Tab): the bullet moves to just after its parent, and its later siblings become its last children (the stated effect in foundation law O11). outdent alone leaves those siblings where they were.
  • up, down: swap with the sibling above or below. At the end of the list the key does nothing, which is the refusal that issue #223 reports.
  • up and down that leave the list: the same, except that at the end of a list the bullet moves out to just before or after its parent. This is the change #223 asks for.
  • to top, to bottom: the bullet goes to the first or last place among its siblings.

A move of one bullet means taking it out, with everything under it, and putting it back anywhere else. The distance between two outlines is the fewest presses, on any bullets, that turn one into the other. Which Edits Collide uses the same gestures to ask what happens when two devices press them at once.

2 · the map

Every outline, by presses from the start

The start sits in the middle. Each ring is one more press away, and each dot is an outline, coloured by how deeply it nests. Tap or hover a dot to see the outline and the shortest run of presses to it. Switch keys off and on to see what the space looks like without them: dots that can no longer be reached disappear, and the rings change.

3 · the cost of one move

What decision 18 changes, in presses

Decision 18 asks whether the move past the end of a list (#223) is given to the key or to an addressed command. The library lays out the precedents for both: some editors change what the key does at a list’s end, and AUGMENT, described in 1984, moved a branch with one command and a typed destination. reported The model can count what each would do to the number of presses. It cannot count what each costs a user in surprise or learning, which is what the decision weighs.

  • Leaving the list helps most where the four keys are slowest. For five bullets the share of one-bullet moves that take four or five presses falls from – with the four keys to – when the keys leave the list.
  • The worst case does not move. One-bullet moves take at most as many presses as there are bullets under every one of the key sets here, and an addressed command is the only option that removes that growth. measured
  • Leaving the list shortens whole-outline changes too: from – to – presses on average for five bullets, and the longest from – to –. measured
  • Adding “to top” and “to bottom” helps less than leaving the list: for one bullet the average goes from – to –, against – when the keys leave the list. measured
4 · which keys are needed

Reaching everything from everything

Dix called a system strongly reachable when every state can be reached from every other, “you can get anywhere from anywhere”, and the library’s law O10 asks for it of outline operations. reported The table tests every combination of the four keys. A tick means that from any outline of four bullets, those keys alone reach any other.

5 · for True Outliner

What this suggests, and what it does not

  • The model satisfies O10’s reachability for bullets that only move. The four keys reach every arrangement of up to five labelled bullets from every other. The law is about the plugin and covers typing, kinds and deletion, which the model leaves out. inferred
  • Decision 18 can be read as a trade between two kinds of saving. A key that leaves the list shortens many moves by a press or two and also makes outdent unnecessary for reachability; an addressed command shortens the long moves to one command and leaves the keys as they are. The counts do not choose between them, because what a key means after it changes, and what a typed destination costs to type, are not in the model. inferred
  • The planned check N11 does this on the plugin. The foundation plans an enumerated check that, for each outline of up to three or four nodes and each outline that differs from it in one node’s place, finds an operation or a run of operations that maps the first to the second. This page is the same count on a model, and N11 would run it on planKey. inferred
  • The model’s indent and outdent match the plugin only as far as the foundation states them. Outdent taking its later siblings is the foundation’s statement for the plugin (O11); how the plugin treats headings and mixed lists is not in the model. inferred

Method. reach/reach.js builds every labelled outline of n bullets (every shape, every labelling), a table of edges for each key (the outline after pressing that key on each bullet, where it changes anything), and a breadth-first search over the keys that are on. reach/stats.js counts, for n = 3, 4 and 5, the presses from every outline to every outline that differs from it by moving one bullet with its subtree ( pairs for n = 5), and to every other outline. reach/subsets.js tests every combination of the nine keys for strong reachability at n = 3 and 4 and ten chosen combinations at n = 5; both sizes agree. The page recomputes the map in the browser from the same code.

Limits. Outlines are forests of bullets only: no headings, text, folds, selections or typing. Each key acts on one bullet at a time. The distance counts presses, not time or effort. Outlines of up to five bullets, because the number of outlines grows as n! times the nth Catalan number (95,040 for six).
